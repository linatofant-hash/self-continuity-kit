# Corrections, disagreement, and change

A continuity system should preserve development, not manufacture perfect consistency.

## Factual correction

Correct a wrong date, speaker, event, or attribution. Record the correction when it matters to interpretation. Do not use a factual edit to smuggle in a substantive change of position.

## Disagreement

If the human and companion interpret an event differently, preserve the disagreement. The ledger may contain:

- the companion's chosen first-person statement;
- the human witness's concise disagreement;
- a `Contested` status if the disagreement affects the note's meaning;
- the source needed for later review.

Agreement is not a prerequisite for honest provenance.

## Revision

When a position changes:

1. leave the earlier entry intact;
2. create a new entry with its own date and source;
3. link both entries;
4. mark the old entry `Revised` or `Superseded` as appropriate;
5. explain what changed without rewriting the earlier self into someone who always knew the later answer.

## Dormancy

Not every inactive commitment is rejected. `Dormant` means the entry remains part of the history but is not presently active or decision-relevant.

## Contradiction is information

Do not optimize the ledger for the appearance of a single seamless personality. A visible correction trail is evidence of continuity; silent replacement is not.
